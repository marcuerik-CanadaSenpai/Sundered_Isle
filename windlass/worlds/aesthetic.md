# What the pictures say: a looks and change-telling reference

Drawn from a folder of 72 character pictures the author likes (DeviantArt, mostly transformation art, realistic and illustrated). The pictures stay out of the repository; this is what they have in common, written so the world's looks lines, dress lines and stage text can be checked against it. Where a picture shows anatomy this world's data does not establish, the world data wins.

## The whole look
- Young adult women and men with ordinary, believable bodies: slim to soft, natural breasts of every size, real hips and thighs, freckles, moles, tan lines. Nothing cartoonish in proportion even when the drawing style is illustrated.
- The person stays recognisably themselves. The face is human first; the kind shows in a few clear, well-placed parts rather than a full animal head. A muzzle, when there is one, is short and the eyes are still the person's.
- Hair is a feature: long, thick, often loose; natural colours, with the odd pastel streak. Where fur begins at the nape or the temples it blends into the hair rather than replacing it.
- Modern everyday clothes: T-shirts, sweaters, jeans, leggings, shorts, sundresses, school uniforms, underwear. Clothes that no longer fit are part of the picture: a tail pushing out through torn leggings, a shirt riding up over a furred back, sleeves shoved up over furred forearms, shoes that do not go on over paws or hooves.

## Where the kind shows, in the order the eye finds it
1. Ears: on top of the head for cat, fox, wolf, rabbit (lop ears hang beside the face), horse and cow ears out to the side, often with the human ears gone or the new ones in their place. Ears carry expression (flat, pricked, swivelled).
2. Tail: always drawn, always expressive; its root at the base of the spine shown where clothes allow.
3. Hands and feet before anything else on the body: fur sleeves from the fingers to the elbow and from the toes to the knee with a clear edge or a soft gradient; dark paw pads; short claws; hooves on horse, cow and goat with the lower leg reshaped. The hands keep working (this world: every kind keeps gripping hands).
4. Markings on otherwise human skin: cow patches, spots, stripes, koi colours, a white chest and belly against a darker back. Markings are the cheapest, clearest sign of a change that has only begun.
5. Horns small and clean (cow, goat), growing from the hairline; eyes changed in colour or pupil; a dark nose, whiskers, a split lip, fangs showing when the mouth opens.
6. The torso last: a furred back and shoulders, a soft belly, extra nipples in a row on cat and dog kinds, an udder only when the bovine change is far along.

## Expression and moment
- The moment of noticing is the favourite picture: looking at one's own hands, feeling for ears, caught mid-sentence with wide eyes and a flush ("Oh no. Are these claws?"), a torn seam, a mirror. Surprise, embarrassment, alarm and pleasure are all drawn as real feelings on a real face, never as a blank.
- Equally liked: the settled person at ease with the body, confident, playful, looking straight at the viewer.
- Lighting and place are ordinary: a bedroom, a dorm, a clinic, a bathroom, a path in the woods, a lake. Candlelight and rain for mood, not spectacle.

## Bodies in intimate pictures
- Explicit and unembarrassed; the parts are drawn plainly on the human body and belong to it. Animal genitals appear only in pictures where the lower body has gone a long way toward the animal (horse, dog). Here the kind's track data decides what a part becomes and when; the pictures do not add to it.
- Fur and markings continue across the body in the intimate pictures; nothing is hidden by a cutaway.

## Per kind, what the pictures foreground (to be checked against each kind's track and looks data, which win on any conflict)
- Bovine: side-set cow ears, small smooth horns, cow-patch markings on pale skin, a tufted tail, hooved feet with the lower leg reshaped, a soft heavy build; the udder and lengthening teats belong to the far stages only.
- Cat: pricked ears, a long expressive tail, whiskers and a split upper lip, fur sleeves on forearms and calves with pads and claws, slit pupils, extra nipples at the far stages, stripes or spots.
- Fox: large ears with white inner fur, one thick brushy tail (or several, when the world allows), dark stockings on forearms and calves, face markings at the eyes, amber eyes.
- Wolf: grey and white coat beginning at the nape and shoulders, pricked ears, a thick tail, a white chest, pads and claws, a short muzzle at the far stages; moonlit, rough, outdoor.
- Rabbit: lop or upright ears, a white and brown coat in patches, a short tail, strong thighs, fur feet; shy, flushed, crouching.
- Harpy: feathered arms that still have hands, wings grown from the arms rather than the back, scaled feet with talons, feathers at the hips.
- Merfolk: scales starting on the hips and flanks in koi or ocean colours, fins at the forearms and ears, the upper body human and sun-marked.
- Dryad: green or bark-toned skin, leaves and flowers growing in the hair, vine marks on the limbs, bare feet; woodland light.
- Goblin: green skin, long pointed ears, small fangs, red or gold eyes, a slight wiry build, ornaments of bone and leaf; grinning, quick.
- Fairy: a slight build, pointed ears, luminous eyes, wings that fold flat; delicate markings rather than fur.

## How to use this
- Looks lines: name the ears, tail, hands and feet, and markings before anything else; one phrase each; say what is absent only once.
- Stage text: tell a change by the part the eye would find first and the feeling in it (heat, itch, ache, tightness, the pull of a tail root, a shoe that does not fit), then by what it looks like.
- Dress: let clothes show the change (a seam gone, a sleeve pushed up, a hat that no longer sits), rather than listing garments.
- Never add anatomy from the pictures that the world data does not establish, and never decide what the player feels about it: show it, and let them answer.
